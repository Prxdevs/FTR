01 Why it’s hard The challenge

Proven before it opens.

A pharmaceutical plant answers to a regulator, not just a client. Every system must be provably validated before the facility can operate, and the records must be good enough to survive an audit.

That means documented Three documented checks: installed correctly, operates correctly, performs correctly over time. checks, cleanroom behaviour as strict as in a chip fab, and compliance with WHO, USFDA and EU Good Manufacturing Practice: the rules that make medicines safe to produce. guidelines.

One team
6 shared disciplines

Planning, quality, safety, 3D design, commissioning and contamination control run across semiconductor, data-centre and pharma work, so one trained team serves all three.

Where the standard comes from

Europe sets the standard here, and Ireland is the main reference, recognised worldwide for pharmaceutical engineering. The key transfer is validation discipline taught to audit-ready standard before the first live project, not learned under regulatory pressure.

04 Questions Answered plainly
What is GMP?

Good Manufacturing Practice: the rules that make medicines safe to produce. Facilities must be built and proven to meet them.

Why does the same team work on chips and pharma?

Both depend on the same disciplines: planning, quality control, safety, 3D design, commissioning and contamination control. Training one team model is more reliable than rebuilding skills sector by sector.

Which standards do you build to?

Compliance runs to WHO, USFDA and EU GMP guidelines, with European, and particularly Irish, practice as our reference.
