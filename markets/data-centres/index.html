Judged on what survives, not what runs.

We build and commission the power and cooling inside hyperscale data centres — and test them by making them fail, before any customer data goes live.

01 Why it’s hard The challenge

Uptime is the product.

A data centre earns money only while it stays on. It is not accepted because its systems run; it is accepted because they keep running when things are made to fail: a power cut, a generator start, a loss of cooling.

That takes backup power designed so no single failure matters (A backup design with one spare unit beyond what the load needs, so one failure changes nothing. or A backup design with a complete second system, so a whole side can fail and the hall stays on.), batteries and generators that hand over in the right order (Uninterruptible power supply: batteries that carry the load for the seconds before generators start.), cooling that keeps pace with dense racks, and a full round of Deliberately causing failures, such as a power cut or lost cooling, to prove everything responds as designed. before any rack goes live.

1.8 GW

India’s operational data-centre capacity in the first half of 2026.

Where the standard comes from

Ireland hosts 82 operational data centres, with 14 more under construction (2024). Irish-led firms deliver 430+ MW of IT load across seven European countries for AWS, Google, Microsoft and Meta. The testing and quality heritage built there, in firms like Mercury Engineering, is the standard this work is built against.

02 What we deliver Scope

What we deliver.

Five things, from the first cable to the final test.

  • Critical power

    High- and low-voltage distribution, switchgear, busbar, Uninterruptible power supply: batteries that carry the load for the seconds before generators start. systems and standby generators, built as A backup design with one spare unit beyond what the load needs, so one failure changes nothing. or A backup design with a complete second system, so a whole side can fail and the hall stays on. and load-tested.

  • Cooling

    The cooling units that chill the air in a data hall. cooling units sized for rack density, and liquid cooling for AI-density halls.

  • Controls and safety

    Control software that monitors and runs a building’s equipment from one place. control systems, fire and gas detection, and the controls that tie them into commissioning.

  • Failure testing

    Deliberately causing failures, such as a power cut or lost cooling, to prove everything responds as designed., with A matrix proving that every possible fault triggers the right response. proven end to end before a rack goes live.

  • Full commissioning

    All five commissioning levels, through to turnover and operational readiness (see the ladder below).

L1 · Factory and site acceptance

Critical equipment (switchgear, UPS, generators, chillers) is tested and witnessed against specification before it is accepted on site.

L2 · Pre-commissioning

Static checks before anything is switched on: continuity, insulation, pressure, cleanliness and calibration.

L3 · System commissioning

Each system is started up on its own and proven against the design.

L4 · Integrated systems testing

Everything together under full load and deliberate failure: power loss, generator start, UPS transfer, cooling loss. This is the level that decides a data-centre programme.

L5 · Turnover and readiness

Indexed records, certification, punch-list closure, operations training and a readiness review.

We plan Level 4 from the first baseline, as a verification event, not a discovery event.

04 Questions Answered plainly
What does “commissioning” actually mean?

It means proving a finished facility works as designed before it is handed over. For a data centre that is five staged levels of testing, from factory checks to a full-load failure test.

What is the difference between N+1 and 2N?

N+1 adds one spare unit beyond what the load needs. 2N builds a complete second system, so a whole side can fail and the hall stays on.

Can FTR handle AI-density cooling?

Yes. Our scope includes liquid cooling for AI-density halls, alongside conventional precision air cooling.
